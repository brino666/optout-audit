Does this even apply to your site? What this tool does and doesn't do

Does the law actually apply to your business?

Not every website is covered. Under California's CCPA, a for-profit business only has to comply if it meets at least one of these (as of 2026):

  • Annual gross revenue over $26,625,000 (global revenue, not just California)
  • Buys, sells, or shares personal information of 100,000+ California consumers, households, or devices per year (this counts cookies and device IDs, not just customer accounts)
  • Derives 50% or more of annual revenue from selling or sharing personal information

If none of these apply to you, CCPA's opt-out requirements likely don't either — a missing or broken opt-out link on a small site under these thresholds is not automatically a violation. Other states with similar laws (Colorado, Connecticut, Virginia, and others) have their own, sometimes different, thresholds. This tool doesn't evaluate your thresholds for you — it only checks the technical mechanism, assuming the law applies. Whether it actually applies to your business is worth confirming with a privacy attorney, not assumed either way.

What this tool checks

  • Whether an opt-out / "Your Privacy Choices" link exists and is reachable
  • Whether the site honors the Global Privacy Control (GPC) browser signal
  • Whether tracking requests actually stop after completing the opt-out flow
  • Friction in the process itself — CAPTCHAs, legal declarations, forced account creation, multi-day wait windows
  • Whether a CCPA request system and a separate cookie-consent banner appear disconnected from each other

What this tool does NOT do

  • It is not legal advice, and a clean scan is not a compliance guarantee
  • It cannot verify whether an opt-out preference persists over days or weeks — only a single point in time
  • It cannot confirm delivery of confirmation emails (e.g. an opt-out request that requires clicking a link in an email) — only that such a step exists
  • It never submits forms, clicks consent toggles, or checks boxes that would change real settings — every scan runs in a fresh, isolated, anonymous session
  • It does not determine whether your business meets the legal thresholds above

Why doesn't this site have its own opt-out link?

Because there's nothing to opt out of. This site runs no analytics, no ad pixels, and sets no tracking cookies — nothing here is sold or shared for advertising, so there's no "sale or sharing" for CCPA's opt-out right to apply to. Payment (when added) is handled via a redirect to a payment processor, not embedded tracking.

What this checks

  • Link presence — is there a working "Do Not Sell/Share" or privacy choices link.
  • GPC signal — does the site actually stop ad-tracking requests when the browser sends the Global Privacy Control header.
  • Opt-out flow — clicking through the real flow and checking whether tracking requests stop afterward.
  • Form friction — does opting out require handing over new personal info first.

This is an automated technical check, not legal advice. It does not determine whether a given state's law applies to your business. A clean result isn't a compliance guarantee — consult a privacy attorney for that.
